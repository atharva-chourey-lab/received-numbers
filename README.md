# Received numbers: a bearer-by-bearer audit of famous figures in Indian history

## Abstract

Famous figures in Indian history circulate detached from the texts that first carried them: a number is repeated until its bearer, its scope, and its evidence class are forgotten. This paper audits a defined set of such figures bearer by bearer, asking who first stated each one, what exactly was counted, and whether an independent record can check it. The instrument is the Chinese-correlation record — Tang and Song annals, translation colophons, and dated pilgrim reports — graded under rules registered before any case was examined. A flagship case study assembles all 50 graded quantities for Nalanda: the celebrated 10,000 is carried by Huili's biography of Xuanzang (c. 665–688), counting residents and visitors together, while Xuanzang's own *Records* say "several thousand", Daoxuan reports more than 4,000 residents, and Yijing reports more than 3,000 residents; the excavated plant (205 plaque-counted cells in 6 of 11 vihāras; an estimated 450–650 cells in total) gives a design capacity of 450–650 residents and an absolute ceiling below 2,000, and the defensible peak is c. 2,500–4,500 residents, centred on 3,000–3,500. Two pre-registered campaigns then grade 37 further cases: Campaign 1 returns 5 corrected, 3 corroborated, 5 bounded and 2 no-bite verdicts; Campaign 2 returns 2, 4, 8 and 8. Every verdict, including every no-bite, is published with the strongest objection to itself.
